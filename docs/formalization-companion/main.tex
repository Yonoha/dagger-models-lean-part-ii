\section{The original Theorem C}

We retain the notation of Part~II.
The category $\prism_{\rev}$ has the order-preserving and order-reversing maps,
and
\begin{align}
  \sSpace^{\dag}_{\rev}
  :=\Fun(\prism_{\rev}^{\mathrm{op}},\sSet).
\end{align}
The symbol $\sSpace^{\dag}$ denotes the actual-index presentation over
$\prism_{\dag}$.
The localizing families are $S_{\rev}$ and $\widetilde S_{\rev}$,
as in the manuscript.
All four model applications below have the original Lean value universe zero.

\begin{theorem}[Theorem C of Part II]
The following four adjunctions are Quillen equivalences,
with each line displaying its left adjoint first:
\begin{align}
  \frakC_{\dag}:\sSet^{\dag}_{\Joyal}
    &\rightleftarrows\sCat^{\dag}_{\Bergner}:N_{\dag},\\
  \delta:\sSet^{\dag}_{\Joyal}
    &\rightleftarrows
      L_{S_{\rev}}\sSpace^{\dag}_{\rev,\inj}:i_0^{*},\\
  \mathrm{id}:L_{\widetilde S_{\rev}}\sSpace^{\dag}_{\rev,\proj}
    &\rightleftarrows
      L_{S_{\rev}}\sSpace^{\dag}_{\rev,\inj}:\mathrm{id},\\
  u_{!}:L_{\widetilde S_{\rev}}\sSpace^{\dag}_{\rev,\proj}
    &\rightleftarrows
      L_{u_{!}(\widetilde S_{\rev})}\sSpace^{\dag}_{\proj}:u^{*}.
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

The second line is the dagger Joyal--Tierney equivalence of
\texttt{jt.thm.main}.
Together these lines give the zigzag of \texttt{jt.cor.goal} and the introductory
Theorem~C.
The manuscript places the categories in a different order;
in that zigzag the first and third displayed rightward arrows carry the
\emph{right} adjoints.
The four lines here preserve the same adjunctions and make their directions
explicit.

\section{Substantial supporting results}

The following propositions describe the checked route used to discharge the
original raw ordered-unit premise.
They refer to the exact constructions in the indicated Lean modules.
The ordinary auxiliary constructions are not substituted for the manuscript's
dagger models or for its final statement.

\needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}[Ordinary Joyal--Tierney comparison]
The actual ordinary two-index realization and native-nerve adjunction is a
Quillen equivalence from the specified ordinary complete-Segal-space model
to the original Joyal model on simplicial sets.
The realization and right adjoint are the fixed functors of the development.
\end{proposition}
\Source{DaggerModels.PartII.OrdinaryJTCSSQuillenEquivalence.isQuillenEquivalence}
{OrdinaryJTCSSQuillenEquivalence}
The argument reuses the checked constant--first-row comparison and a Quillen
equivalence cancellation theorem.
It is an implemented proof of the ordinary input required here,
not an appeal to an unformalized ordinary Joyal--Tierney theorem.

\needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}[The corrected precompletion unit]
For an injectively fibrant ordinary Segal space $W$,
the unit from $W$ to its specified corrected vertical precompletion is a weak
equivalence in the ordinary complete-Segal-space localization.
The unit is a monomorphism.
The monomorphism conclusion itself does not require the Segal hypothesis.
\end{proposition}
\Source{DaggerModels.PartII.OrdinaryPrecompletionUnitCSSWeak.unit_cssWeak;
  DaggerModels.PartII.OrdinaryPrecompletionUnitCSSWeak.unit_mono}
{OrdinaryPrecompletionUnitCSSWeak}
The proof compares the actual horizontal evaluation and constant-section maps
and uses the existing horizontal weak-equivalence recognition.
Completeness of $W$ is not assumed.

\needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}[Complete Segal replacement]
Let $W$ be an injectively fibrant ordinary Segal space.
Let $R$ be injectively fibrant and let
$\operatorname{precompletion}(W)\to R$ be rowwise weak.
Then $R$ satisfies both the Segal condition and the completeness condition.
The specified coherent comparison into this same $R$ is a Kan--Quillen weak
equivalence.
\end{proposition}
\Source{DaggerModels.PartII.OrdinaryPrecompletionReplacementComplete.replacement_completeSegalCondition;
  DaggerModels.PartII.OrdinaryPrecompletionReplacementComplete.replacement_coherentComparison_weak}
{OrdinaryPrecompletionReplacementComplete}
The argument uses the actual coherent diagonal range and its original vertex
preimage, rather than replacing the comparison by another weak map.

\needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}[A chosen complete-Segal-space completion]
For an injectively fibrant ordinary Segal space $W$,
the chosen injective factorization of the terminal map of its corrected
precompletion gives a complete-Segal-space-fibrant object
$\operatorname{completion}(W)$.
The actual composite unit
$W\to\operatorname{completion}(W)$ is a trivial cofibration in the ordinary
complete-Segal-space localization.
\end{proposition}
\Source{DaggerModels.PartII.OrdinaryChosenCSSCompletion.completion_cssFibrant;
  DaggerModels.PartII.OrdinaryCSSCompletionExtension.unit_cssCofibration;
  DaggerModels.PartII.OrdinaryCSSCompletionExtension.unit_cssWeak}
{OrdinaryChosenCSSCompletion; OrdinaryCSSCompletionExtension}
The factorization and lifting interfaces are reused from Mathlib and the
retained model-category infrastructure.
No functorial universal completion API is claimed by this proposition.

\needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}[Extension through the chosen completion]
Let $W$ be an injectively fibrant ordinary Segal space and let $Z$ be fibrant
in the ordinary complete-Segal-space model.
Every map $W\to Z$ extends through the chosen completion unit.
If the given map is a complete-Segal-space weak equivalence,
the extension is weak as well,
with its composite equal to the given map.
\end{proposition}
\Source{DaggerModels.PartII.OrdinaryCSSCompletionExtension.exists_extension;
  DaggerModels.PartII.OrdinaryCSSCompletionExtension.exists_weak_extension}
{OrdinaryCSSCompletionExtension}
The extension is obtained by the actual model-category lifting square.
An unspecified comparison map is not assumed as an additional premise.

\begin{proposition}[Ordered mapping-fiber recognition]
Let $W$ be injectively fibrant and Segal,
let $Z$ be complete-Segal-space fibrant,
and let $f:W\to Z$ be a complete-Segal-space weak equivalence.
For every ordered pair of vertices of $W$,
the literal chosen ordered mapping-fiber map induced by $f$ is a
Kan--Quillen weak equivalence.
\end{proposition}
\Source{DaggerModels.PartII.OrdinarySegalCSSWeakOrderedRecognition.cssWeak_chosenMappingMap_weak}
{OrdinarySegalCSSWeakOrderedRecognition}
The hypothesis requires no completeness of $W$.
The proof passes through the chosen completion and applies recognition between
complete-Segal-space-fibrant objects.
The composition comparison for chosen fibers is made after Kan localization.

\needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}[The exact raw transpose]
For every original reversal-space input,
the curry of the exact original raw transpose is weak in the ordinary
complete-Segal-space localization.
Its actual raw right-nerve target is complete-Segal-space fibrant.
The first assertion does not require locality of the reversal-space input.
\end{proposition}
\Source{DaggerModels.PartII.RawOrdinaryJTUnitCSSWeak.rawTranspose_cssWeak;
  DaggerModels.PartII.RawOrdinaryJTTargetCSSFibrant.rawTarget_cssFibrant}
{RawOrdinaryJTUnitCSSWeak; RawOrdinaryJTTargetCSSFibrant}
These statements refer to the same original transpose and target that occur in
the ordered raw-unit application.

\needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}[The original raw ordered-unit premise]
For an original reversal-local input and every specified original ordered pair,
the literal map \texttt{rawChosenFiberMapAtP} is a Kan--Quillen weak equivalence.
Consequently the exact original premise
\texttt{OriginalRawOrderedUnitWeak} holds without additional assumptions.
\end{proposition}
\Source{DaggerModels.PartII.TheoremCOriginalCompletionProof.rawChosenFiberMapAtP_weak;
  DaggerModels.PartII.TheoremCOriginalCompletionProof.originalRawOrderedUnitWeak}
{TheoremCOriginalCompletionProof}
Forgetting reversal supplies injective fibrancy and the Segal condition.
The previous two propositions and the existing endpoint calibration then
discharge the original premise.
Applying the retained original recognition interface proves Theorem~C.

\section{Verification, reuse and review scope}

The final proof uses 359 named stage modules.
Their original local Lean builds succeeded and their sources and imported
artifact hashes were checked recursively.
The saved stage logs contain 1,182 printed axiom reports,
all using only \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and
\texttt{Quot.sound}.
In particular the main theorem and each of its four literal component targets
have only these standard axioms.

Every one of the 156 directly imported project roots passed a Lake
content-rehash/no-build gate,
allowing Lake to check its transitive source graph.
This source-freshness gate did not recompile the whole graph.
The final two files were also recompiled from a saved source snapshot.
The separate-repository publication checks are recorded with the release;
they do not amount to a cold rebuild of the entire new repository.

The Lean toolchain is \texttt{leanprover/lean4:v4.27.0}.
The inherited Mathlib revision is
\nolinkurl{a3a10db0e9d66acbebf76c5e6a135066525ac900}.
Part~I supplies the dagger Joyal and dagger Bergner infrastructure and their
existing equivalence.
Mathlib supplies model-category factorization and lifting,
localization, simplicial and groupoid infrastructure,
and other foundational APIs.
Existing attribution is retained.

Public receipts omit private machine paths and original command strings.
They preserve the source and log hashes,
unchanged axiom-log bytes,
artifact and import hashes,
actual exits,
durations and original receipt hashes.
They are explicitly labelled normalized exports.

The literal four-link audit is not an independent Mathlib-only Challenge.
No new Linux CI or Comparator replay with negative controls is claimed.
An AI correspondence review found no concrete mismatch in the reviewed
original target;
this does not constitute independent human review.
Nor does the final proof establish a general total-derived-functor API or
complete every intermediate claim of the older finite-support alternative
route.

The source repository includes a manual workflow,
which is not automatically triggered for this publication.

use the dedicated release tag or an exact commit rather than a moving branch.
